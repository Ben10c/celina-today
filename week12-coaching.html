Tuesday - 20 minutes

ESB: introduction and the poem by heart

Your role: AUDIENCE. Listen first. Prompt only if she dries, and don't recite it for her.

Start with a short introduction, then she recites the poem by heart, with expression and rhythm. This section should last about 2 minutes.

Introduction

She says the title and anything else the booklet asks for, clearly and unhurried.

The poem

Off by heart, with expression and rhythm. If a line is shaky, loop just that line, then run the whole poem again.

4P's and body

Watch posture, stillness, and how she uses her voice. The booklet on Classroom has the school's notes.

Finish line: one clean run of the introduction and poem, from memory, in about two minutes. Pack the library folder — 4.8 Library Session is today.

Cheetah talk tips (from the 11 Nov review)

Add a conclusion

The talk still needs a clear ending. Even Merit needs a beginning and an ending, so finish with why cheetahs need protecting, then thank the audience and invite questions.

Point at the slide

Mark the script where she points at each picture, and point in every section: title, appearance, habitat, and diet.

Eye contact and voice

Look at everyone in the room, not only the examiner. Vary her voice — excited on the speed fact, serious on the threats.

Likely questions

Practise answers to questions such as "Why are they so fast?" and "How can we help them?"

Chinese — printed sheet, 10 min

The Chinese teacher sent home a printed Home Learning sheet on Monday. She spends about 10 minutes on it tonight. The completed sheet goes back on Monday 23 November; the answers are shared after it is returned. Leave the writing to her.

Friday - 20 minutes

ESB book extract, then questions

Your role: AUDIENCE, then QUESTIONER. Listen to the reading first. After the talk, ask a few questions and let her answer.

Section 2, Book extract. She practises her introduction, then reads 5 pages with expression. In the exam, the examiner chooses exactly which part they want to hear, so she should be ready for any stretch of those pages.

Introduction

Title, author, and anything else the booklet asks for, said clearly before she starts reading.

Five pages

Expression first, not speed. If one page is flat, go back to it, then continue.

Questions

Ask a few questions after the talk and let her practise answering confidently. Don't supply the answers. For the cheetah talk, useful ones are "Why are they so fast?" and "How can we help them?"

If there is time: run the poem once more. Keep thinking about the 4P's and body in every part.
